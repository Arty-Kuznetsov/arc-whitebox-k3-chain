### 1. Exact band entry at flopscope's truncated-SVD rate: re-price the parked k3k construction, not the compression
- **verdict:** promising — closed by: none. §54 parked k3k with an explicit reopen condition, 'worth doing only if a cheaper entry update is found', and this is that update. §58's mechanism (the construction costs what it saves) is answered here because the construction is re-priced from the code, not assumed.; rules OK: True; construction priced: True
- **mechanism:** Lens verdict first. The engine halves the output count M only when it can prove the output is symmetric, either because operands alias (inner(A,A), outer(v,v)) or because an operand is tagged with as_symmetric (_einsum.py / _accumulation/_cost.py: the Cat A/B/C candidates). None of the 23.4% of B spent on legs and D can use this. A leg transport Wt·X is never symmetric. D is needed in full, not just its symmetric part: K21 uses D and D^T with different coefficients, and Yp = d1 D^T d3 + d2 D d2. The polarization trick (XY^T+YX^T from two half-billed Grams) returns only the symmetric part, at the price of one product. float16 bills 1.0, the same as float32. take, compress and 3-argument where all bill at weight 4. So the dominant products already sit at their billing floor. The one rule that does price below the algorithm we use is linalg.svd(M, k=): it bills min(4mnk, economy) for an exact top-k, where the kernel computes the economy SVD and slices. The doc calls this an 'accepted residual'. The construction that sank §54 is exactly a top-r subspace problem. The band basis is the top-r left singular subspace of F = [P·diag(S), A_e, B_e], since F F^T equals the band Gram. After every entry the band Gram in the new coordinates is diag(S^2), so no Cholesky or eigh of M is ever needed. One call svd(F, k=320) returns (1) the new basis P' = U, (2) the entrant's coefficients P'^T A_e and P'^T B_e as column slices of S·Vt, at no extra cost, and (3) the rotation R = P'^T P = S·Vt[:, :r]·diag(S_old)^-1. H stays outside the band as a thin leg. The quantity that changes is the per-entry-layer construction term: k3k's listed ≈4–7u (two Gram applications 2.75u, 2 QR 0.42u, eigh 0.18u, new coefficients 0.63u, rotation ≤1.8u) becomes ≈1.5–1.9u. The accuracy is the already-measured exact top-r arm, not a cheaper approximation.
- **cost (proposer):** Unit u = one dense n×n×n product = 2n^3 = 0.098% of B plain (0.062% at the 0.636 Strassen billing). Per entry layer: svd(F,k=320) on a 1024×2368 F = 4·1024·2368·320 = 3.10e9 = 1.45u plain. Column scaling P·diag(S) is O(nr). Rotation done lazily is P_cur·R_g at 0.1u per band source per layer (≈4.5u per MLP); done eagerly it is 0.2u per band source per entry (≈9u per MLP). New coefficients and the new M come free. ### 2. One-sided projection of the old tail's D pairing on a free transported Jacobian basis (legs untouched); proxy says marginal

### 3. Symmetric-aware C_pre: the only provably symmetric product in the chain (exact, bundle-only)
- **verdict:** marginal — closed by: none. §10/§49/§56 concern Strassen recursion depth, not output symmetry.; rules OK: True; construction priced: True
- **mechanism:** C_pre = Wt·C·W, and Wt·W at layer 0, is the only product whose output is symmetric by construction, so it is the only place the symmetric-output rule can legitimately apply. Keep P = Wt·C in the leg stack as now. Then compute P·W only on the block upper triangle, three levels deep: diagonal blocks recurse, off-diagonal blocks go through the existing rectangular Strassen, and the lower blocks are mirror copies billed n^2/4 per level. That saves 1/4 + 1/8 + 1/16 = 7/16 of the second product. The engine's own route, a 3-operand einsum('ki,kl,lj->ij', W, C_sym, W) with W aliased, bills the second step at half. But it loses Strassen on the first step: 1.5u against 1.27u now, so it is worse. The quantity that changes is the second C product.
- **cost (proposer):** Second product ≈0.636u per layer drops to ≈0.36u. The saving is 0.28u × 15 layers ≈4.2u ≈0.41% of B. The cost is 3 extra Strassen invocations per layer (≈60 flopscope calls each, about 20 µs per call), roughly +40–80 ms local residual against k3m's 0.197/0.227 s. That may break the ≤0.2 s local shipping rule.
- **projection (proposer):** util 24.835% → 24.42%: 5.3906e-9 × 0.983 ≈ 5.30e-9. Below the ≈2–3% grader resolution, so worth shipping only alongside a bigger lever.
- **skeptic's re-pricing:** The atlas puts the second C_pre product at 0.96% of B (15 bare units at 0.636, layers 0-14; the last layer is diagonal only). Three levels of upper-block-triangle recursion save 7/16 of it, 0.42% of B, so util goes 24.835% -> 24.42%, x0.983, about 5.30e-9. Mirror copies bill about n^2/4 per level (negligible), and Strassen addition overhead on the (m x 1024)(1024 x m) sub-blocks stays about 1%, so the 0.636 billing holds. This is an exact, accuracy-neutral FLOP cut, so unlike accuracy levers it is deterministic and verifiable locally; §60's resolution argument does not apply. It is still below 3%. The unpriced cost is residual time. The off-diagonal blocks of one level can be batched as strided views, but _Strassen sizes its top-level workspace for G=1: X3 at level 0 is (7,1,512,512), so LA = X3[:7G] needs G <= 1. So either enlarged G-batched workspaces (more RSS), or up to 15 invocations per layer (about +0.4 s, fatal). With batching: +3 invocations x about 90 ops x 20 µs x 15 layers, about +80 ms local, taking k3m's 0.197/0.227 s to about 0.28/0.31 s. One level only saves 1/4 (0.24% of B) for about +27 ms.
- **skeptic's reason:** (1) Not closed. (2) Allowed and clean: output symmetry is genuine reduced computation, which §5.5 explicitly encourages. (3) FLOP construction is priced correctly, but the residual cost (+80 ms with 3 levels batched) is the real price and pushes the estimator past the local 0.2-0.25 s shipping rule. (4) x0.983 is a certain gain but under 3%. Ship it only if a bigger lever (for example the svd-priced band or the column cut) leaves residual headroom. Then prefer a 1-2 level version, which saves 0.24-0.36% of B for 1-2 extra invocations.
- **cheapest falsifying test:** Bill the block scheme on one layer in a BudgetContext (FLOPs are exact, no MLP needed), and time the 3 extra calls on the Mac. Drop it if the residual cost exceeds 30 ms.

### 1. Oseledets (Benettin) band: the shared-subspace tail with a transport-only basis and no Gram/eigh/refinement at entry
- **verdict:** promising — closed by: none (§54 closed k3k on the cost of its entry update and parked exactly this branch: 2026-09-25_cost/RESULTS.md §7 says 'if a cheaper entry update is found (e.g. q=0 validated in torch)'. A Gram-free q=-1 was never run. The B-only sub-arm IS closed by §22.); rules OK: True; construction priced: True
- **mechanism:** Replaces the ENTRY UPDATE of the k3k band (NEGATIVE §54), the one term that made it more expensive than dense. The band legs A_s, B_s are all right-multiples of the same Jacobian product J_{l,m}. By Oseledets for products of random matrices, the dominant left subspace of J_{l,m} is shared by all old m, and it is reached by TRANSPORT ALONE: P_l = W_l^T D1 P_{l-1} (Benettin iteration, rows cut and gated like a leg). A leg that already lies in span(P) stays there exactly, so after entry the coefficients α_s, β_s are fixed. Only the entrant needs a projection. The design keeps P UNnormalised, with only a free diagonal column rescale, so no coefficient rotation (r²n per leg) is needed. The entrant's coefficients come from a least-squares solve against the r×r Gram P^T P. There is no band-Gram application, no Rayleigh–Ritz eigh, and no rotation of 18 legs. QR plus a rotation run only when cond(P^T P) passes a float32 threshold. Materialisation (P α_s), the compressed pairing Z = Σ L1 β^T + L2 α^T followed by Z P^T, and the readout are identical to the validated k3k port. Only the basis update changes. Second arm: a B-only band from a0 = 4 (NEGATIVE §22: B is the tolerant leg, B-only rank 256 at age 4 is far milder than A), with A kept dense, plus the full band from a0 = 6.
- **cost (proposer):** Anchored on MEASURED k3k (27.78% vs k3j 26.62%), whose entry was about 4–7 plain units/layer: 2.75 Gram, 0.42 QR, 0.18 eigh, ≤1.8 rotation, 0.63 new coefficients, 0.3 P transport. Benettin entry at r = 320, in plain units per band layer: P transport r/n = 0.31, P^T[A_e, B_e] = 2r/n = 0.63, Gram P^T P + r³ solve ≈ 0.1, amortised re-orthonormalisation ≈ 0.1–0.3. Total ≈ 1.1–1.3 plain units per layer, against 4–7 today. That saves ≈ 3–6 units × 10 band layers = 30–60 units = 2.9–5.9 points of B, so k3k-Benettin ≈ 21.9–24.9% against k3j 26.62% (×0.82–0.94). ### 1. Slice-exact sliding drop: when a source ages out, its (3,) and (2,1) slices go into the newborn's correction leg (the r = 0, O(n^2) tail format)
- **verdict:** closed — closed by: §62 ((t, D) is not a sufficient kappa3 state; all-distinct part 28.8%/layer, closure must capture >=93%), with §46 (memoryless chain, all-distinct pass-through ~23% of |t'|), §16 and §58 Stage A (tail tolerance 3-5% relative); rules OK: True; construction priced: False
- **mechanism:** Right now a source is carried at 4 products per layer for its whole life, and the window (§16) instead drops it completely, losing its t, D and all-distinct content together. This idea changes one step. At the layer where source s reaches age a0 (after its last full readout), drop s but compute the newborn's correction from what is still carried: dK3 = K3 - d1^3 (t - t_s) and dK21 = K21 - d1_i^2 d1_j (D - D_s) - tree - paths. The post-activation tensor at that layer then has EXACT (3,) and (2,1) slices. The only thing lost is the all-distinct part of G^{x3} X_s at the drop moment. The absorbed slices form the slice-supported tensor Sym(V x I x I). It sits on the newborn's own hub leg A, so it is then TRANSPORTED by the chain's own rule for the newborn's remaining a0 layers (no closure, no fit, no basis), and it regenerates all-distinct content at every later layer. Content older than a0 is re-sliced once every a0 layers. What changes: the per-layer cost of every source of age >= a0 goes to ~0, while t_old and D_old at the drop layer stay exact. For comparison, §58 Stage A shows that dropping them costs x41.8 (t) and x26.5 (D). Nesting: purely additive into the newborn's dK3 (a vector) and dK21. Production: the t part is free. For the D part, D_s must be summed apart from the rest (its pairing is computed anyway: route it to its own accumulator). Fit the FREE alpha-family row by row (beta_i = <D_s^post[i,:], (C0 D1)[i,:]> / ||(C0 D1)[i,:]||^2, then alpha += beta; this is the part already carried free as 3B diag(alpha)). The amplitude-like piece v_j var_i is rank 1. The remainder goes into R ahead of the existing thin range finder.

### 2. Oseledets band: a transported (never re-fitted) shared basis. Each source is projected once at entry, and the projection residual's slices go into the newborn's correction leg
- **verdict:** closed — closed by: §54 (shared subspace k3k: ideal 6:256 +7.7%, 4:384 +8.7%), §22 (A-leg projection loses coherently; per-source optimal rank 256 from age 4 gives x1.67), §62 (the slice fix cannot recover the all-distinct part), plus the §6 spectrum numbers; rules OK: True; construction priced: True
- **mechanism:** Q (n x r) is initialised once from the first entrant's legs. After that it is only transported (Q <- G W^T Q), with a QR each layer for conditioning. A source reaching age a0 is projected ONCE onto span(Q), after its last full readout. Because W^T maps span(Q) onto span(W^T Q) and gating is diagonal, band legs stay in the band exactly for the rest of the chain: A_s = Q Z_s, with Z_s fixed up to the QR rotation. There is no re-projection, no band-Gram application and no Rayleigh-Ritz, which is the part that cost k3k 4-7 products per entry. Why the basis should be good (Oseledets): the left spectrum of a product concentrates (§6: age 4, top-256 = 97.5%) onto a subspace set by the RECENT matrices, and a long-transported Q converges to the same subspace. The known failure of projections is §22's coherent loss (the discarded A adds up coherently in t). It is removed at entry by absorbing the residual's (3,) and (2,1) post-slices into the newborn's dK3/dK21, exactly as in the slice-exact drop. What changes: cost per band source-layer drops from 4 to 4r/n + 2(r/n)^2. Nested: yes (one projection per source; the span is invariant). This is the r > 0 rung of the same ladder whose r = 0 end is the slice-exact drop, and the fallback if the all-distinct content matters but lives in the fast Lyapunov subspace.
- **cost (proposer):** Per band source-layer: materialise Q Z_A and Q Z_B (2r/n), compressed pairings L1 Z_B^T and L2 Z_A^T (2r/n), rotation 2(r/n)^2. That is 1.125 units at r = 256, billed ~0.78 (rectangular Strassen, level 2) with alive rows ~0.85, so ~0.75 units, against ~1.88 for a full source at k3m. Per layer: Q transport r/n; QR ~0.13; the final Xi Q^T r/n; entry Q^T[G A_e, G B_e] 2r/n; residual materialisation 2r/n; the projected entrant's pairing 2r/n; D residual into R ~0.3. That comes to about 2.4 units/layer. Totals: (a0, r) = (4, 256) is 107 (young) + 58 (78 band source-layers) + 23 (overhead) ~ 188 units = 18.4%. (3, 256) is 80 + 68 + 25 ~ 17.0%. (6, 320) is about 22%.
- **projection (proposer):** (4, 256) at delta = +2%: 2.1652e-8 x 1.02 x 0.184 = 4.06e-9 (x0.75). (3, 256) at delta = +3%: 2.1652e-8 x 1.03 x 0.170 = 3.79e-9 (x0.70). It does not reach the floor alone, because the band still costs ~0.4 of a full source per layer. It is a real, validatable step (well above the 2-3% grader resolution), not first place.
- **skeptic's re-pricing:** The basis, QR, entry projection and materialisation are priced honestly (~2.4 units/layer). The flaw is the rank. By Eckart-Young, no shared basis captures an entrant better than the entrant's own top-r SVD, and §6 puts that at 97.5% for top-256 at age 4. So (4,256) fails the idea's own 99% stop rule by construction; (3,256) fails worse. Passing needs r >= 384. Then a band source-layer costs 4(0.375)+2(0.14) = 1.78 plain units (~1.18 billed) against 1.88 for a full source, and overhead rises to ~3.4 units/layer, so util is ~23-24%, which is no saving. At r=256 the loss without the fix is >= x1.67 (§22, and that uses an optimal per-source basis). The slice fix recovers at most ~71% of only the first-step error (§62). Estimated raw >= x1.2-1.5 -> 2.1652e-8 x 1.2-1.5 x 0.184 = 4.8-6.0e-9, i.e. x0.9-1.1 of today, not reliably a gain.
- **skeptic's reason:** Same shared-subspace mechanism as §54. It drops §54's entry update, and the entry update is what made the basis fit the entrant. Removing it saves construction but gives up the capture. The Oseledets premise is misapplied: the entrant's dominant subspace is set by its last a0 random matrices, which are independent of Q's history (their right singular vectors are Haar-random relative to it). So the transported Q captures the entrant like a zero-power randomized range finder, and that is below the top-r SVD bound, which is itself already below 99%. The discarded A-part adds coherently in t (§22). The entry slice fix leaves the residual's all-distinct content lost (§62: 28.8% per step, orthogonal to anything cheap). The 'D residual into R ~0.3' also assumes a low-rank residual, which is unverified and contradicted by §21/§58.
- **cheapest falsifying test:** (1) Diagnostic, minutes, one chain pass. Along the chain, measure the capture fraction ||Pi_Q A_e||^2 / ||A_e||^2 (and the same for B_e) with Q transported from its first entry, for (a0, r) in {3, 4, 6} x {128, 256, 384}, against the entrant's own top-r SVD capture. Dead if below 99% at (4, 256). (2) The same k-step open-loop oracle as the slice-exact drop, with r > 0, with and without the slice fix. (3) 10 MLPs, new switch band=(a0, r, slicefix). Run (6, 256), where shared_sub without the fix is +7.7%. Falsified if the fix does not bring it to <= +2%. Then run (4, 256) and (3, 256).

### 3. Feed-form carrier for the amplitude mode: add a fitted vector v_s to the newborn's P2 hub h. This is 'vector x chain-transported D1C0D1', free by construction
- **verdict:** closed — closed by: §62 §5a (star ansatz Sym(u x C0) with an oracle per-neuron u captures 18.8% of the all-distinct part, 10.6% at L8 to 26.7% at L14; all five basis vectors 19.5%; >=93% needed), with §46 (amplitude form R^2 0.09-0.39) and §39; rules OK: True; construction priced: True
- **mechanism:** The P2 feed is already a nested O(n^2) tensor format in production. It is 3Sym(h x M), with M = D1C0D1 at birth, carried by the chain's own pass-through rule as J M J^T = B diag(d1b) A^T. It is read out as t += 3 m o q and D += 2 diag(m) B diag(d1b) A^T + q m^T, and the first D term rides the existing L2 A^T product. Anything added to h is therefore carried for free, and all additions merge (h stays one vector). Positive homogeneity says an old source's content has an amplitude component Sym(v x C). Its diagonal part is slice-supported (so the slice-exact drop already absorbs it). Its off-diagonal part is exactly 3Sym(v x D1C0D1) to leading order. So when a source ages out, fit a per-neuron v_s and set h_newborn += v_s. Its birth (2,1) slice 2(v d1)_i C0_ij d1_j lies in the free alpha-family, and its t slice is 0 because M_ii = 0. Slices therefore stay exact, and this piece carries the COHERENT part of the all-distinct content that the slice-exact drop throws away. It reads at the next layer as 3 (W^T v)_i (W^T M W)_ii, with the matrix partner carried by the chain's rule. There is no Delta = C_post - G C_pre G drift, which a closure on the current C would have. Use it as the add-on arm between the slice-exact drop (r = 0) and the Oseledets band (r > 0).
- **cost (proposer):** Carrying: 0 (the existing P2 machinery; h is already a transported vector). Fit per layer, two options. (a) Row least squares of D_s^post against 2 d1_i C0_ij d1_j: O(n^2). (b) The Frobenius-optimal v: the right-hand side A_s(g o diag(B_s^T M B_s)) + 2 B_s(g o diag(A_s^T M B_s)) needs M B_s (1 product); then run CG on (||M||_F^2 I + 2M^2) with M-matvecs, O(n^2) per iteration. That is about 1.1 units/layer, roughly +0-1.1% util on top of the slice-exact drop's figure (a0=3 about 8.5-9.6%, still at the floor).
- **projection (proposer):** Enabler, not standalone. If it halves the slice-exact drop's delta at a0 = 3: 2.1652e-8 x (1 + delta/2) x 0.10 = 2.17e-9 (1 + delta/2), i.e. the floor at half the accuracy price. At a0 = 4: 2.40e-9 (1 + delta/2).
- **skeptic's re-pricing:** The carrying cost really is zero: the drop layer equals the newborn's birth layer, so 3Sym(v x D1C0D1) rides the newborn's own B diag(d1b) A^T, and h stays one vector. The Frobenius-optimal fit is ~1.1 units/layer (~+0.1% util). Accuracy: §62's readout-optimal oracle LS on exactly this family (a free n-vector times the C0 partner, pre-gating equivalent of D1C0D1) is an upper bound on what any Frobenius-fitted v achieves on the readout. That bound is 19-27%, below the idea's own kill threshold (30%) and far below the >=50% needed to halve the slice-drop error. So at best it removes ~20-25% of an error that is itself x3-x20 at a0=3 (see the slice-exact drop). Projection: no gain over today at any a0.
- **skeptic's reason:** The mechanism (put the all-distinct remainder into the form Sym(v x C0-like matrix)) is the star closer that §62 already fitted as an oracle and found capturing <=27% even at the deepest layer. Fitting per source and transporting it, instead of refitting per layer, does not enlarge the family on the readout; at one layer all dropped content shares the same M. It is an enabler for the slice-exact drop, which is itself closed, and its dead rule (<30%) is already met by the published number.
- **cheapest falsifying test:** Inside the slice-exact drop's k-step oracle (no extra harness). (i) Measure the fraction of the dropped source's all-distinct Frobenius norm captured by 3Sym(v x M) (Gram form, as in 2026-09-26_old-tail/stage_b_gram.py). Dead if below 30%. (ii) Measure the reduction of the k = 1, 2, 4 tail errors in t and D against slice-only. Keep only if it is at least 2x at a0 in {3, 4}.

## Lens: Statistics, sketching and hybrid estimators (`stat-sketch`)

### 1. Saturated-ROW readout cut of the D pairings, with the kappa4 level restored exactly in O(n^2). The saturated-side counterpart of the dead-row cut, applied to the readout only
- **verdict:** promising — closed by: none; rules OK: True; construction priced: True
- **mechanism:** Quantity changed: the rows i of the D-pairing output D = sum_s [L1_s B_s^T + L2_s A_s^T] (plus the path and feed terms that ride on it) for neurons that are saturated at the current layer (alpha_i = mu_i/sigma_i > a_sat). Only the left-factor rows are cut. Leg rows, D columns and t_i are all kept. Why the rows carry almost no information: a saturated neuron is close to identity, so the pass-through legs already carry kappa3[i,i,j] exactly. I went through every consumer of D_ij in nonlin_slices, _nonlin and the birth code and wrote down the weight each one puts on row i. Every weight decays with alpha: (a) Q11 (the covariance) has 0.5*d2_i*d1_j, so phi(alpha); (b) the C0*D term in Q11 has w31_i, so alpha*phi; (c) dK21 = K21 - d1^2 d1 D - tree has D_ij*d1_j*[d1_i(1-d1_i) - P1_i d2_i], also about alpha*phi. In K21 the O(1) part d1_i d1_j D_ij cancels exactly against the pass-through subtraction. (d) The path births Y enter through d2_x and d3_p. (e) K3 minus d1^3 t cancels. The one consumer with an O(1) weight is the post-kappa4 (2,2) slice: K22o[i,j] contains [d1_i*alpha_j - 2(P1 d2)_i (P1 d1)_j] D_ij. It enters the chain only through the scalar level (Ssum, trM). A level shift compounds (k4mult 1.05 costs x2.8), so this term must be restored.
